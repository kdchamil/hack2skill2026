1. Executive summary
FiveSeconds is a camera-first AI shopping companion. It turns any food, cosmetic or personal-care pack into a spoken, visual, evidence-graded answer in about five seconds, in the shopper's own language. It gives one A to E grade, and never an opaque one: six cards beneath it explain why, say how sure each finding is, and suggest what to do next, for the shopper and for every member of their household.
Problem. Labels across Asia-Pacific are dense, multilingual, often imported, and written for regulators rather than shoppers. Existing scanner apps rely on barcode databases that are thin outside Europe and North America, give one verdict per product, and cannot read a pack they have never seen.
Solution. Gemini multimodal models read the pack itself (front, back, ingredient list, dates, batch code), whether or not a barcode match exists. They then fuse that reading with open product, recall and regulatory datasets. Every finding is labelled Verified, Label-stated, Inferred or Unknown.
Why it matters. It helps households managing allergies, diets or skin concerns, along with low-literacy and older shoppers and carers buying for others. Better information at the shelf is one of the cheapest public-health levers available.

2. Product vision and design principles
Vision: point, reveal, listen, explore. Any shopper, in any language, can understand any pack in five seconds and make a choice that fits their household's body, budget and values.
North star (17 Oct): the Curious and Conscious Shopper. Make shoppers curious about what they put in their basket, and conscious of its impact on their own health, each person in their household and the planet. Every feature must serve one or both.
What is novel
	•	Label-first, not barcode-first. Gemini reads the physical pack, including ingredient lists, nutrition panels, dates, batch codes and claims, in any script. Products missing from databases still get a full answer. That gap is common for local and imported goods across JAPAC.
	•	Explain every grade. One A to E grade for the glance, never shown alone. The deck's six factors become the six summary cards beneath it, each with a plain verdict, a reason, a tier and a next step. With too little data, no grade is shown.
	•	One scan, whole household. Each family member gets their own verdict chip on the same scan, for example "Avoid for Amaya: contains sesame." The match runs on the phone, so profiles never reach our servers.
	•	Voice-first and conversational. The shopper can listen to the summary and ask follow-ups aloud: "Can my dad have this with his low-salt diet?" This serves low-literacy and older users as first-class users.
	•	Claims truth check. Words like "natural", "clean", "sugar-free" and "dermatologically tested" are tested against the ingredient list and any available evidence. The gap is shown plainly.
	•	Commercially neutral by design. There are no sponsored rankings. Price appears only after the shopper declares a budget, and then as 3–5 options nearest to it.
	•	Shelf compare in one photo. The shopper can photograph 2–4 products side by side and compare them on their own priorities.
	•	Remember what happened after. A private, dated product-use diary links a skin reaction, an odd taste or smell, or a damaged pack to the products used before it. It then guides a report to the right authority, such as the Consumer Affairs Authority in Sri Lanka (finale).

Gemini reads, normalises the long tail and writes. Plain code looks up, calculates, matches and decides (Section 6.0).

5. Competitive differentiation
The five leading scanners all work the same way: barcode in, database lookup, one score out. FiveSeconds reads the pack itself and returns evidence-graded findings for each household member, in the shopper's language and by voice. It is also built for Asia-Pacific, where the leader is largely absent. Yuka's 2026 press kit lists 15 countries; Australia is the only one in JAPAC.

Not advertised by any of the five: recall and batch checks, claims verification, a confidence tier on each finding, read-aloud commentary, and verified labour-risk evidence. These five gaps are FiveSeconds' core.
The newer wave: AI photo-label scanners
By 2026 many small apps, such as NutriEye, Eat IQ and Label AI, were reading label photos with AI. They prove the demand, but they still output one score or a list of flags. None advertises evidence tiers, household verdicts, recall checks or multilingual voice output. FiveSeconds' edge is the trust model (Section 10) and the household and voice experience, not OCR alone.
Halal verifiers, calorie trackers and Open Food Facts
Six more products overlap with parts of FiveSeconds. Label-photo reading is no longer novel on its own; our edge is the combination of household verdicts on the phone, evidence tiers, local-language voice and the diary-to-report loop.

6. Functional requirements
The core engineering rule is the AI reads and explains; deterministic code calculates and decides. Gemini extracts structured fields and writes commentary. Versioned, unit-tested rules compute %RI, allergen matches, risk categories and verdicts. This keeps every number reproducible and testable.
6.0 Where AI earns its compute
Gemini is used at four points in a scan, plus report drafting at the finale: wherever the input is unstructured or the output is language. Everything else is plain code, which is cheaper, faster and testable. Each AI step is followed by a deterministic guard, and Section 12 measures what the AI adds over a rules-only baseline.
Step
Why plain code is not enough
What the AI does
What plain code does next
1. Read the pack
OCR returns text, not meaning. It cannot tell the ingredient list from marketing copy, or per-100 g from per-serving columns, across thousands of layouts, curved packs and 10+ scripts. Templates break on the next pack.
Gemini Flash reads 1–4 photos into the typed schema (FR-04), with a legibility score per field.
Validates the schema; fields below 0.6 become Unknown; parses and compares dates.
2. Resolve unusual ingredient names
A dictionary covers common names, but labels use synonyms, translations, OCR noise and brackets: "whey powder (milk)", "කජු", "芝麻", "E322 (soy)".
Maps only the terms the dictionary misses to canonical allergen, additive and diet IDs, with a reason.
Matches IDs to allergens, diets and additives. New mappings are reviewed before entering the dictionary.
3. Test claims
"Sugar-free" or "no added MSG" needs the claim's meaning and the ingredients that would contradict it.
Classifies each claim and names the evidence lines.
Applies regulatory thresholds in code where a rule exists; otherwise the result stays Inferred.
4. Explain and speak
Templates across 7 languages, 6 cards and every finding combination read like forms, worst in Sinhala and Tamil.
Writes ≤ 12-word headlines and one-sentence reasons from the typed findings only; Gemini-TTS speaks them.
Checks allergen and medical terms against the glossary; rejects any number not present in the findings.
5. Diary to report (finale)
A note such as "rash on her arm after the new soap" must become the fields an authority asks for, in a formal register.
Extracts the fields and drafts the report, only when the user asks.
Routes to the authority; the user reviews and sends.
6. Turn pasted text into a list
Free text mixes languages, quantities, brands and generic names ("kiri powder 400g x2"). Rules cannot tell a brand from a product range or split run-on lines reliably.
Gemini Flash returns typed items: name, quantity, unit, preferred brand, canonical product range. Only the list text is sent: no profile, no history.
The phone saves the list after the shopper's review; range matching on later scans is plain code.
Never AI: barcode decoding, Open Food Facts lookup, %RI arithmetic, allergen and diet matching against profiles (on the phone), expiry checks, verdict words, tier assignment, recall-match thresholds, encryption, consent and hash checks, and authority routing.
Compute per scan:
	•	A repeat scan (same barcode and label hash) is served from cache with 0 model calls.
	•	A first scan makes one Gemini Flash vision call; the dictionary runs first, so only unmatched terms go back to the model.
	•	One small text call writes the commentary; audio is cached per language.
	•	Gemini Pro is called only when no rule can settle a claim.
How we prove it. Section 12 runs the golden set through two pipelines: Cloud Vision OCR with the dictionary and regex rules, and the Gemini pipeline. It reports allergen recall, field accuracy, latency and cost per scan side by side, so "why not plain logic?" is answered with numbers.

9. Solution architecture on Google Cloud
The system is one stateless Cloud Run API. Inside it, an ADK agent sends images to Gemini for reading, calls deterministic tools for lookups and rules, and streams typed findings back to a PWA. The phone matches findings to household profiles, so profiles never reach the API. Every component is a managed Google Cloud service.

Meta-profile. Alias (for example "Amaya" or "Child 1"), life stage, allergen IDs, diet IDs, skin type and goals, all from fixed lists. No real name, birth date or free text.
Keys.
	•	A random AES-256-GCM data key encrypts everything on the phone. It is stored only in wrapped form.
	•	Where the phone supports the WebAuthn PRF extension, a passkey with fingerprint or face check yields the wrapping key through HKDF. Support is good on Android with Google Password Manager and on iOS 18.4 or later; elsewhere a 6-digit-or-longer PIN derives the key, and the app says this is weaker.
	•	A 128-bit recovery code, shown once, wraps a second copy of the data key so a new phone can restore the backup. Passkeys that sync through Google or Apple restore it too.
	•	The native app (after the finale) moves the key into Android Keystore or the iOS Secure Enclave with biometric binding and normal OS backups.
Backups. Browser storage is not part of phone backups and can be evicted. The PWA asks for persistent storage and offers an encrypted export the user saves to their own Drive, iCloud or Files.
Sharing protocol (finale).
	•	The owner taps Share on a member. The invite (link or QR) carries a one-time token and the owner device's key fingerprint.
	•	The recipient sees exactly which fields will be shared and taps Accept. Their device label and public key go into the consent record.
	•	The owner's device encrypts the profile to the recipient's key (HPKE with X25519) and uploads the envelope.
	•	After each edit, the owner's device raises the version, computes HMAC-SHA-256 over the canonical profile with the share-group key, and uploads new envelopes and the hash. The server pushes "changed" only to recipients whose status is active.
	•	The recipient's device decrypts and recomputes the hash. On a mismatch it keeps the old copy and warns.
	•	Revoke stops envelopes and pushes at once and rotates the group key for the remaining recipients.
	•	Request removal pushes a delete instruction. The recipient's app deletes its copy and confirms (Removed); no confirmation in 7 days shows Not confirmed. A recipient can also leave at any time.
Why a keyed hash. A meta-profile has few fields from short lists, so a plain SHA-256 could be reversed by trying every combination. An HMAC under a key only the devices hold cannot.
Honest limits. Someone who has seen a profile can remember or screenshot it. Removal is a request the app honours, not a guarantee, and the UI says so. Adults own their own profiles; a parent or carer may hold a child's or a dependant's.
10. Evidence, confidence and trust model
Every finding the user sees is a typed object with a source and a tier. The UI cannot render a claim that has no tier.

Meaning
Example

"Recall notice from [regulator] matches this batch"

"SPF 50 (label claim)"

Not readable or no reliable data
"Labour practices: no reliable data"

Rules
	•	Never upgrade a tier. An Inferred finding can never be shown as Verified, and the AI never invents a source.
	•	Ground before you claim. Recall, regulatory and labour findings require a retrieved document. The model's own knowledge alone yields at most Inferred.
	•	Keep risk categories distinct. The categories are Prohibited in your market, Restricted (with limits), Allergen, Debated / controversial online, and No known concern.
	•	Wording guardrails. Suggest, never diagnose ("may", "consider"). Never say "safe for you". Medical-adjacent cards end with "Check with a professional".
	•	Recall matching. Product name + brand + batch + market gives High confidence. Name + brand only gives Medium, shown as "Verify the match before acting". Anything less is not shown as a match.
	•	Authenticity score (R2). A 0–100 score built from listed signals: GS1 prefix vs stated origin, barcode-record match, label completeness, registry match and print-quality cues. It always carries the caveat that a barcode cannot prove a product is genuine.
	•	The A to E grade. Computed by versioned rules from the six cards (FR-47). The model never sets a grade.
